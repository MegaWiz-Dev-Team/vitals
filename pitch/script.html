02

What it actually is

0:20 – 0:55 · 35s
On screenSlide 02 — the product: talk to her, treat her, she lives or she does not, the run is provable.

Four things happen in one run. You talk to her — she answers in her own words. You treat her — drugs, airway, fluids, on a monitor that moves the way real physiology moves.

She lives or she does not. There is no script. A deterministic clinical automaton decides, from what you did and when you did it.

And the run reduces to one hash. Anyone can replay the tape and get the same answer — without asking us.

04

Why it matters

1:20 – 2:00 · 40s
On screenSlide 04 — one doctor ≈ 1,400 years of life · density table · 1% = cutting attrition by a tenth.

Our mission is one per cent more doctors reaching patients every year. Here is what one per cent is worth.

A study across three thousand US counties found that ten more primary care physicians per hundred thousand people added fifty-one days of life expectancy. Work that back to a single doctor and you get roughly fourteen hundred years of human life.

In Indonesia there is one doctor for every 2,150 people. In Japan, one for every 370. The marginal doctor in Jakarta stands in front of six times the people.

And one per cent is smaller than it sounds: nine per cent of medical students never finish, and the biggest single reason is that the work was too hard. One per cent means saving one in six of them.

06

The part that makes it a protocol

2:35 – 3:20 · 45s
On screenSlide 06 — off-chain/on-chain diagram, then the live validator REJECTING an over-claim.

The model makes it playable. The automaton makes it provable.

The tape and the scenario are the only inputs. The chain never sees a run — only a leaf that has to prove against a root it built itself.

Watch what happens when someone claims a level they did not earn. Claim “Proficient” — rejected. The program recomputed it as Competent from three distinct attempts and refused. Claim “Competent” — granted, and what gets stored is what the program computed, not what was claimed.

No issuer key. No oracle. The program refuses its own user. Every other achievement system mints because a server said so. Ours mints because the chain checked the arithmetic.

07

How it is paid for

3:20 – 3:55 · 35s
On screenSlide 07 — free to the learner · relying parties pay · donations earmarked on chain.

It is free to the learner, in every country, forever.

We tested charging. Eighteen faculties use it and none of them bought. The buyer is the learner — and charging the learner would aim this at the countries that need it least. That is a mission constraint before it is a business one.

So money comes from the people who rely on the record: employers and residency programmes verifying a candidate, and a take rate on the case marketplace. Donations are the base — and they are earmarked by the program. Money given for a scenario can only be spent on that scenario, and it is released when the delivered content hash shows up. A funder recomputes our impact instead of reading our report.
