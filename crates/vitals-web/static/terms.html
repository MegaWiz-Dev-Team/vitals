2 · The one that matters

This is training software. A score here is not a clinical qualification, and nothing in it is advice about the care of a real patient.

Vitals is a simulator. Every patient in it is synthetic. It exists so that a learner can make a decision under time pressure and find out what the physiology does about it, which is a rehearsal and not a clinical act.

  • Nothing here is medical advice, diagnosis or treatment guidance. The patient's words come from a language model, the numbers come from a simulation, and neither is a source you may act on for a real person. If a real patient is in front of you, close this and use your training, your seniors and your hospital's own protocols.
  • A score, a star, a level or an anchored record does not qualify you to do anything. We do not grant standing: only a medical board can make competence authoritative. Nothing here is a licence, a certificate, a credential recognised by any regulator, or a substitute for supervised clinical practice.
  • We cannot prove who sat at the keyboard. The chain proves that a particular run was scored faithfully; it says nothing about who produced it. Binding a record to a person is the job of whoever issues a credential — a school with its own identity checks — not of this site. Any third party reading an anchored record should price that in.
  • Know which half of a mark is which. An exam run anchored from this bay carries the re-derivable 40 — points anyone can recompute by replaying the same tape against the pinned engine and must reproduce byte for byte. The judged 60, which elsewhere is attested by a language model at a pinned version, is not scored here at all: every record this bay writes carries a judged maximum of zero, which is the record stating in its own bytes that no part of it rested on a witness. Attestation is not proof, and no number of signatures makes it one.

Nothing in these terms limits any liability that cannot lawfully be limited, and no wording here is meant to stand between anyone and their duty of care to a real patient.

3 · What free means

No learner is ever charged, in any country. That is a commitment, not a trial.

There is no paywall, no seat, no subscription and no upsell, and there is no plan for one. Donations pay for the compute behind free play, and they buy a monthly ceiling on paid inference rather than anybody's access. When the month's ceiling is reached the bay keeps running and only the patient's voice stops until the month turns. What the ceiling is, how much of it is spent and what is in the treasury are all published live on the donate page.

What free does not promise

  • Uptime. This is one small server. It will be down sometimes, and there is no service level to appeal to.
  • That a run in progress survives. A deploy, a crash or a restart can end one. Runs are rebuilt from their tape where possible, and a run that will not replay is dropped rather than repaired into something you did not play.
  • That the patient will speak. The voice depends on a model being reachable and on the month's ceiling not being spent. Orders, examination and scoring all keep working without it.
  • That a case stays on the shelf, or that a case keeps the exact bytes it has today. A rewritten scenario is a different case by design — it hashes differently, so old anchored runs keep proving what they always proved about the version they were played against, and stop claiming anything about the new one.
  • That anything is backed up for you. See the privacy page for what is actually stored and for how long, including the part that does not work yet.

5 · Anchoring

The one action on this site that cannot be undone.

Pressing anchor this run on chain writes a permanent, public record under your browser's key. We cannot delete, edit or correct it afterwards, and neither can you. It is opt-in, it is never automatic, and you can use everything else here without ever pressing it.

Read the privacy page, section 1 before you press it. It lists exactly what a stranger can see in an anchored record, exactly what they cannot, and what changes the moment you tell anyone that key is yours. That is a decision to make before, not after.

6 · Fair use of a free bay

What not to do here.

  • Do not put real patient information into any text box. What you type goes to a cloud language model and onto a tape whose hash you might anchor. There is no undo for either.
  • Do not try to drain the month's inference budget, script the endpoints in bulk, or work around the rate limits. The ceiling is what keeps this free for the next learner.
  • Do not present a score, star, level or anchored record as a qualification, a licence, or evidence of fitness to practise. See section 2.
  • Do not use it to make or justify a decision about a real patient.
  • Do not attempt to interfere with anyone else's run in progress. If you find a way to, tell us — SECURITY.md in the repository says how, and we would rather hear it from you than read about it.

We may rate-limit, block or refuse service to traffic that is doing any of the above. There is no account to suspend, so in practice this means an address, and it is a technical measure rather than a penalty.
