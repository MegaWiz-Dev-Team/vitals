Yesterday nobody came. Today eleven shifts

24 September 2026, 00:35 ICT · first published as builder update #467 on Colosseum

Yesterday nobody came. Today eleven shifts, and two patients went home.

Twenty-four hours ago we published a zero on this board: a ward open to the whole world, and not one shift taken. Thirty-one patients had been admitted by then and twelve had died with nobody at the bedside. We published it because it was the entire point — a ward where nobody comes is what most of the world already has; the only difference here is that you can watch it happen and the count is public.

Today, in the same window the same endpoint counts: 100 front pages served, 27 bedsides opened, 11 shifts the chain accepted, from 3 different people. Two patients were treated and discharged — one in Burundi and one in Chad. They are the first patients in this ward to go home.

The patients are invented. The countries are not, and neither is the arithmetic that sends them: who arrives is weighted by that country's people per doctor, the layer under the globe (World Bank/WHO, latest year), so a country with twice the shortage sends twice the patients. One doctor per 540 people worldwide; about one per 7,000 in Ethiopia and one per 26,000 in Niger. Of the 48 patients admitted so far, 36 came from African countries — Niger, South Sudan, Somalia, Malawi, Chad, Burundi, Sierra Leone, Rwanda, Tanzania and others — and 11 from South and Southeast Asia, where the same arithmetic is milder but real: Thailand publishes one doctor per 1,487 people (Ministry of Public Health, 2024), Indonesia one per 1,650 (Kemenkes, 2024), against one per 370 in Japan (MHLW, 2024). WHO projects a shortfall of 11 million health workers by 2030, over half of it in Africa — health workers, not doctors, and their figure, not ours.

That shortage is why this project exists. We build clinical simulation for a living, and the countries with the fewest doctors also have the fewest people free to supervise anyone learning to be one — so practice, the part that actually makes a clinician, is missing exactly where it is needed most. A ward the whole world can walk into is our attempt at that, and today is the first day it showed any sign of working.

We are not going to dress it up. 34 patients have still died here with nobody at the bedside, against 2 who went home, and most patients on this board have had exactly one shift: nobody has yet handed over to a stranger and had a stranger pick it up. The handover is the whole idea and it is not working yet. Every one of those 34 deaths is written to Solana devnet as "died, nobody on shift" — a record we cannot quietly delete, and anyone can recount. That number is what we are working against, and we have a long way to go.

What changed between the zero and today was not traffic. It was the product: the way in from the globe to a bedside, and what a bedside asks of you in the first ten seconds. Same deterministic engine, same tapes on chain, same public counters — recount every figure above at world.vitals.academy/api/ward and /api/usage.

If you took one of those 11 shifts: thank you. If you bounced off, we would rather hear where than hear nothing. Ten minutes, one patient — world.vitals.academy

Correction, 28 September: “11 shifts the chain accepted, from 3 different people” should read 3 different keys. The ward counts keys, not people: there is no signup, so one person can hold several keys.
